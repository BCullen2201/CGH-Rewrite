As an example of the appearance of an actual handwritten will from early 18'th century England, here is a low resolution scan of a portion of the first page of the will of Thomas Cullen. Thomas was born April 4, 1652 to Gervase and Katherine (Robinson) Cullen at Upton, Nottinghamshire. Thomas would later marry Elizabeth Bennet of Bottesford, Lincolnshire, in 1696. Thomas died April 30, 1715. The handwriting and language used in the document gives one a sense of the difficulty encountered when trying to decipher written materials from such early dates. This particular will has fared better than most in the passage of time. Thomas likely did not write or even sign his own will since, as was commonly found, literacy was not something particularly useful to a yeoman farmer. Many thanks to Cliff Cullen of New Zealand for the forwarded copy of the will of Thomas Cullen. Many thanks also to my father, James Cullen, for his amazing work in transcribing the will in its entirety. He has also provided a useful glossary that defines many of the archaic terms that appear in the will of Thomas Cullen. Further details were extracted from the book "The Rude Forefathers" by Francis H. West, as well as from various other sources. Use the links to explore the complete transcription of the will. We hope this exercise is a help to others who may try to decipher wills found for members of their own families. One will find that the language of these documents are similiar in 17'th and 18'th century England as well as on the American East Coast during the same approximate time period.

Will Transcription

The complete text of the Will of Thomas Cullen 1652-1715. Transcription was tedious and at times confusing due to both the archaic language and the usual difficult handwriting. The terms defined in the glossary are superscripted in red with the index number where they appear.

Will of Thomas Cullen, 1652-1715

In the name of God Amen. I Thomas Cullen of Upton near Southwell in ye county of Nott yeoman do make my last will & Testament in manner and forme following. Whereas upon my marriage with my last wife I did surrender one moyety1 or halfe part of all my copyhold2 messuages3 lands tenements4 and hereditaments5 that I then was seised of. To ye use of my selfe and wife in Taile6 Which said Entaile7 I have ye day of the date hereof dockt8 by a Recovery according to ye custom of ye Mannor of Southwell aforesaid so that now I am seized of all my Copy hold estate in ffee9 simple. Therefore I do give and devise the same in manner and forme following (that is to say) All that my messuage in Upton aforesaid now in my possession and all ye houses outhouses crofts10 yards orchards and gardens there unto belonging and all those lands tenements and hereditaments that are mortgaged to Mr Story for one hundred pounds as also all those that are mortgaged to Mr Lowe and ye Equitable right of redemption thereof, and ye church leys11 close12, and Bull close, and all ye arable land hereafter ment(i)oned lying in the East ffeild14 of Upton (vizt.13) two lands on Micle barrow14, ye land of ye heirs of mr. Trueman lying between them, containing one acre, one land on poolands going over ye beck15 containing about three roods16, one land containing about three roods at ye high thorne, one land containing one acre going down to ye springs, one headland at England ditch containing halfe an acre, one land containing halfe an acre at quick springs, two lands cont(aining) three roods called broadarses, one land on little cross hill containing halfe an acre, and one selion17 in the pingle18 containing one rood. And the arable land hereafter ment(i)oned in ye Middle ffeild14 vizt. ffoure lands in peak willow nook containing two acres, ffoure lands near Howbards dale19 cont(aining) one acre, two lands near ye new close side containing one acre, two lands going down to Lamb hedg(e) on red lands containing ffive roods, Gunthorp east and Gibson west, one land on ye cliff containing three roods, ye vicars land lying on both sides. And all ye arable Land hereafter ment(i)oned lying in the West ffeild14 (vizt.) one land cont(aining) one acre on ye Cliffe ffurlong20, ffoure lands on ye same ffurlong containing one acre, one land or lea on Brick Kilne Line lands74 containing halfe an acre, one land in priesting containing three roods, and one ley in Mill holme21 Leys within Greeting being meadow, containing one acre. And halfe an acre in Greeting meadow abutting on ye pouches, halfe an acre in the church meadow near the Bank Mr Alvey east, halfe an acre in ye Bulls mr Jenison south halfe an acre in ye church meadow close ye towns meadow west, halfe an acre at ye far end of ye east meadow Mr Alvey south and ffoure cow gates according to ye old stint22, and all yt23 my close at Bottesford in ye county of Leicester, all and singular which said premisses I give and devise to my oldest son Thomas Cullen his heirs and assigne(e)s charged and changable with ye payment of the severall sums of mon(e)y hereafter ment(i)oned that is to say to each of my ffoure daughters ye sum of ffifteen pounds of good mon(e)y to be paid to them at their respective ages of one and twenty years or marriage, and ten shillings a peice yearly to each of them till ye same be payable. Item24 I give and devise all yt23 my Cottage at Upton town end, and all other any lands tenements and hereditaments in Upton aforesaid or elsewhere, (not before in this my will devised) To my yo(u)ngest son John Cullen his heirs and assigne(e)s, charged and chargable nevertheless with ye payment of ye like sums of ffifteen pounds apeice of good mon(e)y to each of my ffour daughters at their severall ages of one and twenty years or marriage with power for my daughters to enter into all ye afore said premisses for non paym(en)t, and I devise that if any of my daughters dye before their legacys becom(e) due, that her part so dying shall not go to ye other sisters, but be for ye benefit of him out of whose estate it should be paid. Item all my goods cattle and chattells of what kind soever I give to my ffoure daughters equally to be divided amongst them, and if any of them happen to dye before their age of ffourteen years then ye part of such so dying To be equally divided amongst the surviveing sisters. And whereas I have lately borrowed of Mr Andrews of Southwell aforesaid the sum of fforty pounds for Which I have made a Mortgage of severall Lands and tenements therein ment(i)oned Which said Mortgage I do hereby ratify and confirme. I do likewise by this my Will devise and appoint that all my just debts be honestly paid as well those that I do now owe as those that I shall hereafter owe upon Mortgages or any otherwayse howsoever. And lastly I make my eldest daughter Elizabeth, my brother Gervas Cullen, & my cousin William Robinson the yo(u)nger of Nottingham fframe work knitter joint Executors of this my last Will and Testament and do hereby revoake all former wills by me made. In Witness whereof I the aforesaid Thomas Cullen have to this my last Will put my hand and seale the thirti(e)th day of April Anno Dni 1715

Thomas Cullen
Signed sealed and published
by the said Thomas Cullen in
the presence of us, who did in
the presence of ye Testator,
Subscribe our names hereunto

Samuel Lowe
John Hoyland
William Kemp

30 July 1715
Proved before Mr Robert Moore by William
Kemp; and Elizabeth Cullen & Gervas Cullen
are the Executors; William Robinson having
first renounced [his role].

Goods and Chattels of Thomas Cullen

The condicon25 of this obligacon26 is such That if Elizabeth Cullen eldest Daughter of Thomas Cullen late of Upton afores(ai)d yeom(an) dec(ease)d of the age of Eighteen years and the above bounden27 Gervas Cullen...

...do & shall well & faithfully execute & p(re)form the last Will & Testam(en)t of the said Thomas Cullen and pay & discharge all his Debts & Legacies so far as his goods & chattels will extend and as the law doth charge them. And of all the said goods & chattels shall make or cause to be made a true & p(er)fect Inventory and the same so made shall exhibit or cause to be exhibited into the Registry of the above named Chapter and further shall make or cause to be made a true & just Accompt28 thereof When they shalbe thereto lawfully required by the said Chapter or their Comissary for the time being. Then the s(ai)d obligacon shalbe void.

Gervas Cullen
Sealed & delivered
